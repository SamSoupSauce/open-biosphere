## 1. Executive Assessment

Open Biosphere is an unusually ambitious and internally well-structured open-hardware program: nine machines documented across three build tiers (manual â†’ electromechanical â†’ autonomous), four physical zones plus an interactive map, fourteen RFCs, a 128-part canonical registry, a 12-crop biology matrix, and a documentation methodology (RFC-0007/0010/0011/0012/0014) that most hobby projects never attempt. The stage-gated build model, the strict two-control interlock philosophy, and the air-gapped network design are genuinely good engineering instincts.

However, the blueprints are **not currently build-ready as a coherent set**, for three classes of reasons:

1. **Published data corruption.** A text-mangling defect in the MDX sources strips leading characters from dollar figures and breaks LaTeX arrows (`$\rightarrow$` â†’ `$ ightarrow$`) across at least seven machine pages. The [live Mantis page](https://samsoupsauce.github.io/open-biosphere/hardware/mantis-branch-crawler/) currently displays cost figures of "3.00", "+35.00", "28.00", and "05.00"; the values supported by the machine's other tables are $93.00 / +$135.00 / $228.00 / $405.00.
2. **Unreconciled multi-layer cost and specification conflicts.** Machine costs exist in five independent layers (tri-modal matrix, machine index, stage BOM sums, the React catalogue component, README) that disagree with each other in at least five cases, and machine-level specs contradict themselves (voltages, speeds, dimensions, load ratings).
3. **Physical feasibility and safety gaps.** Several headline specifications cannot hold as written: a pump rated 80 W asked to deliver an operating point requiring ~431 W of hydraulic power; a 20 N load cell bound to a documented 850 N compaction path; a 300Ã—450 mm sheet specified to form a 300 mm-diameter drum that needs ~942 mm of circumference. Beyond these, the BCA's power architecture is unreconciled (nameplate loads totaling ~850 W against a listed 250 W supply), and the gantry's 8.5 kg toolhead rating is unsupported against its documented 45 N magnetic retention until a coupling drawing or latch is published.

Additionally, no obtainable implementation of the software stack the Stage-2 documentation depends on â€” the `biosphere-core` controller runtime, the Flutter companion "Virtual Panel" app, and the ESP32 firmware â€” was identified in the inspected repository or its README distribution locations, even though RFC-0004/0005/0006 are marked "Implemented" (F-10).

**Overall verdict:** Strong documentation architecture, weak data integrity, and unresolved core engineering questions at the load-path and power-budget level. Remediation is feasible â€” most defects are source-text and data-layer issues fixable with linting and a reconciliation pass â€” but until the P0 items in Section 10 are closed, a builder following these blueprints would order wrong parts, wire undersized power supplies, and read corrupted prices.

### 2.3 Severity model

- **Critical** â€” a specification cannot hold as written (physically impossible), or a procurement binding defeats the component's documented function.
- **High** â€” corrupted or contradictory published data that would mislead a builder's procurement or wiring; unresolved safety-relevant design questions.

- **Low** â€” cosmetic or editorial.

## 4. Systemic Findings

### F-01 â€” Source-text corruption of cost figures and LaTeX, live on the site â€” **High**

**Evidence:** The [Mantis overview page](https://samsoupsauce.github.io/open-biosphere/hardware/mantis-branch-crawler/) renders its upgrade table as "3.00 / +35.00 / 28.00 / 05.00" where the machine's other tables support $93.00 / +$135.00 / $228.00 / $405.00, and its roadmap headings render as `Base Machine $ ightarrow$ Add-On 1`. The corruption is present in the MDX sources ([mantis index](https://github.com/SamSoupSauce/open-biosphere/blob/fb3c410a930d1dfe41a581b3ba954564d1f79990/src/content/docs/hardware/mantis-branch-crawler/index.mdx), and the same pattern in the ground-track-agv, microclimate-dosing-array, overhead-gantry-system, tps-root-chamber, and aerial-pollination-drone index pages) â€” source-text corruption reproduced on the live page, not a browser rendering bug. The [Mantis stage-2 BOM](https://samsoupsauce.github.io/open-biosphere/hardware/mantis-branch-crawler/stage-2/) shows the same corruption ("6.00", "2.00", ".00", "+77.00", "05.00"), and the [microclimate stage-1 page](https://samsoupsauce.github.io/open-biosphere/hardware/microclimate-dosing-array/stage-1/) contains broken `$CO_2$` LaTeX in its BOM.

**Consequence:** A builder cannot trust any dollar figure or arrow-bearing sentence on the affected pages; values are silently wrong, not flagged.

**Acceptance test:** CI step that parses every MDX table cell matching `/^\s*[\d.]+\s*$/` and fails if a cost column lacks a `$`; plus a grep gate for `ightarrow$`.

### F-04 â€” Physical control hardware banned by RFC-0005 still specified throughout â€” **High**

**Evidence:** [RFC-0005](https://samsoupsauce.github.io/open-biosphere/rfcs/rfc-0005-hardware-interlocks-ble-virtual-panel/) (marked Implemented) bans physical panels, buttons, dials, and potentiometers across all tiers, and its remediation directives order them purged from catalogs and BOMs. Yet:

- The [microclimate dosing array index](https://samsoupsauce.github.io/open-biosphere/hardware/microclimate-dosing-array/) spec table lists a "6-Button Potentiometer Panel" (Add-On 1).
- The [overhead gantry index](https://samsoupsauce.github.io/open-biosphere/hardware/overhead-gantry-system/) spec lists a "6-Button CNC Jog Pendant".
- The `MachineCatalog` component (rendering the [interactive machine catalogue](https://samsoupsauce.github.io/open-biosphere/hardware/catalog/)) ships items including "IP67 Box with Fwd/Rev Jog & Potentiometer", "Rotary Countdown Mechanical Timer Dial", and "Green Tactile Jog Button & Toggle Switch" ([source](https://github.com/SamSoupSauce/open-biosphere/blob/fb3c410a930d1dfe41a581b3ba954564d1f79990/src/components/MachineCatalog.tsx)).
- The [README](https://github.com/SamSoupSauce/open-biosphere/blob/fb3c410a930d1dfe41a581b3ba954564d1f79990/README.md) describes Tier 2 as "tactile push-buttons, rotary countdown dials, toggle switches, and wired pendants".

**Consequence:** The wiki simultaneously tells builders that these controls are banned (stated safety rationale: humidity ingress, control-fight race conditions) and instructs them to buy and install them. Contradictory safety guidance is worse than either position alone.

**Acceptance test:** CI text scan for `potentiometer|jog|pendant|toggle` in machine specs and catalogue data, whitelisting historical/RFC context.

### F-05 â€” Network architecture: RFC-0004's migration was never applied to machine pages â€” **High**

**Evidence:** [RFC-0004](https://samsoupsauce.github.io/open-biosphere/rfcs/rfc-0004-controller-node-tool/) Â§4 explicitly directs: "Strip Monolithic CAN & Onboard Pi Modulesâ€¦ Replace the legacy CAN-bus-only architecture in Add-On 2 across all 8 machines" with the Wi-Fi AP + BLE downlink CNT stack (detailed in [RFC-0006](https://samsoupsauce.github.io/open-biosphere/rfcs/rfc-0006-agnostic-controller-runtime/): `POTFRM-<CLUSTER_ID>` SSID on 192.168.42.0/24, WebSocket `ws://192.168.42.1:8443/register`). But the machine index pages still specify "CAN bus / MQTT" ([sprout bioreactor index](https://samsoupsauce.github.io/open-biosphere/hardware/revolving-sprout-bioreactor/), [AGV index](https://samsoupsauce.github.io/open-biosphere/hardware/ground-track-agv/)), "Master CAN / MQTT Bus" ([microclimate index](https://samsoupsauce.github.io/open-biosphere/hardware/microclimate-dosing-array/)), CAN broadcast (mantis), CAN interlocks (TPS), and the [master-assembly](https://samsoupsauce.github.io/open-biosphere/overview/master-assembly/) integration standard #4 specifies "isolated RS-485 Modbus and CAN bus" with ESPHome/ROS2. The catalogue component likewise lists "ESP32-S3 Dual-Core MCU with CAN Bus" and "IP67 M12 CAN/RS-485 Interlock Harness" items. The machine pages also conflict on compute placement (master-assembly Phase 4 puts a Raspberry Pi CM4 on the gantry; RFC-0004/0006 confine heavy compute to the central controller).

**Consequence:** A Stage-2 builder cannot determine what bus to wire. Local peripheral links (UART/RS-485 to a tool) can legitimately coexist with CNT, but the machine-level coordination text contradicts the mandated architecture. This is a stalled migration, not a layered-design choice, because RFC-0004 ordered the replacement and the machine pages were not updated.

**Acceptance test:** Per-machine "comms" field validated against the RFC-0004/0006 stack in CI.

### F-06 â€” RFC-0010/0011 status is stale; step SVGs are unreferenced â€” **Medium**

**Evidence:** [RFC-0010](https://samsoupsauce.github.io/open-biosphere/rfcs/rfc-0010-stepwise-reverse-deconstructed-blueprints/) claims "All 24 machine stage documentation manuals across the 8 autonomous agricultural machines conform" with "144 SVGs" and a 1:1 blueprint-to-step invariant. The defects are: (a) RFC-0010's "Implemented/Canonical" status and compliance tables are stale as stated; (b) the ninth machine (BCA) post-dates the "8 machines" count and has no stepwise SVG assets (its steps use per-part ModelViewer blocks instead); and (c) some step-level ModelViewer blocks appear mispaired with their step content â€” e.g. the BCA stage-1 band-heater installation step displays the power-supply model rather than a heater ([source](https://github.com/SamSoupSauce/open-biosphere/blob/fb3c410a930d1dfe41a581b3ba954564d1f79990/src/content/docs/hardware/bca-packaging-engine/stage-1.mdx)). **Acceptance test:** Either mark RFC-0010 superseded by RFC-0014 for machine visuals and dispose of or adopt the SVGs, or re-link them.

### F-08 â€” BOM part-ID mismatches: canonical IDs point at the wrong parts â€” **High**

**Evidence:** Per [RFC-0008](https://samsoupsauce.github.io/open-biosphere/rfcs/rfc-0008-global-part-registry-bom-schema/), BOM part IDs are supposed to bind to the canonical registry. In practice, many BOM rows link `PART-MECH-ALUM-2020-6M` (a [2020 aluminum extrusion](https://samsoupsauce.github.io/open-biosphere/parts/mechanical/part-mech-alum-2020-6m/)) for non-extrusion parts: microclimate stage-0/1/2 rows for burettes, needle valves, test kits, backplane, gear sets, and driver boards; mantis stage-1/2 rows; AGV stage-1; and a TPS stage-2 row linking an ESP32-S3 node to an OLED display part ID. BCA stage-1 links `PART-MECH-BELT-GT2-9MM` (GT2 belt) to a row labeled "#40 Roller Chain". The registry page for the extrusion lists $28.00, contradicting the $3.50 unit prices on those BOM rows â€” confirming broken bindings rather than alternative sourcing.

**Consequence:** The registry's core promise (one ID â†’ one part â†’ one price, roll-up-able) fails; procurement tooling following the IDs would order extrusions instead of valves, or trust mismatched prices.

**Acceptance test:** CI validation that every BOM row's part-name text matches the registry entry for its linked ID, and a price cross-check.

## 5. Engineering Feasibility Findings (computed)

### E-01 â€” Skimmer pump: the stated operating point cannot be delivered by an 80 W pump â€” **Critical**

The [skimmer stage-1](https://samsoupsauce.github.io/open-biosphere/hardware/fluidic-skimmer-loop/stage-1/) calibration step states "FLOW: 55 GPM | PRESSURE: 18 PSI | RATED: 80W". Hydraulic power \(P = Q\,\Delta p\): 55 GPM = 3.47Ã—10â»Â³ mÂ³/s; 18 psi = 124 kPa; \(P \approx 431\ \text{W}\). Delivering that flow and pressure simultaneously requires at least ~431 W of hydraulic power â€” more than 5Ã— the 80 W electrical rating before any losses. Either the figures are separate endpoint ratings (max flow at zero head; max pressure at zero flow) or the rating is wrong; as written, the calibration target cannot be achieved. The machine index's "240 W max" pump and "45â€“75 GPM" circulation range add a third inconsistent set of numbers (F-11). **Fix:** publish the actual pump curve and the duty point; align all three pages.

### E-02 â€” BCA Stage 1: unreconciled power architecture (~850 W nameplate load vs 250 W supply); thermal BOM rows missing â€” **Critical**

[BCA stage-1](https://samsoupsauce.github.io/open-biosphere/hardware/bca-packaging-engine/stage-1/) instructions require "dual 300W ceramic band heaters" (600 W nameplate) plus a 250 W geared motor â€” nameplate loads totaling ~850 W â€” while the BOM's only supply is `PART-ELEC-PSU-24V-250W` (24 V / 250 W), with no separate heater supply or load-sequencing scheme identified in the reviewed material. This establishes an unreconciled power architecture (it does not prove a wired system would draw all loads simultaneously, but the documents provide no way to know). Additionally, the heaters, PID controllers, SSRs, and thermocouples that the assembly steps explicitly install do not appear in the stage BOM at all â€” a builder following the BOM would have no heating hardware. And the motor is listed "12V 250W" while the supply and driver context are 24 V (F-11). **Fix:** per-stage power budget table; BOM completeness check against step text.

### E-03 â€” BCA load cell: 20 N sensor bound to a documented 850 N compaction path â€” **Critical**

The [BCA stage-2](https://samsoupsauce.github.io/open-biosphere/hardware/bca-packaging-engine/stage-2/) prose requires an "S-beam 200 kg load cell" for compaction-density feedback with a stated **850 N compaction target**, and its checklist requires calibration stability over thermal cycles. But the canonical part ID bound in both the BOM and the ModelViewer is `PART-ELEC-SNS-LOADCELL-20N` â€” a **20 N miniature bending-beam cell** (the viewer names it "20 N Miniature Bending Beam Load Cell"). A 20 N sensor directly carrying an 850 N load is overloaded roughly 42Ã— its rating: it would be damaged or destroyed, and the compaction-feedback loop it exists to serve cannot function. This is the most dangerous procurement error in the wiki because the prose reads correctly and only the linked part is wrong. **Fix:** instantiate a â‰¥1 kN S-beam cell in the registry and rebind.

### E-04 â€” Gantry: 8.5 kg toolhead rating vs 45 N magnetic retention â€” **High (unresolved)**

The [gantry index](https://samsoupsauce.github.io/open-biosphere/hardware/overhead-gantry-system/) claims "up to 8.5 kg toolhead payload", while the Stage-0 kinematic magnetic base specifies "RETENTION FORCE: 45 N". An 8.5 kg toolhead weighs **83 N â€” 1.85Ã— the stated retention force â€” before dynamic loading** (acceleration, gantry motion, tool reaction forces). Whether this is supportable depends on facts the documents do not provide: coupling orientation, whether 45 N is per magnet pair or total, and whether a positive latch or secondary support exists (none is documented). A dropped 8.5 kg toolhead over a basin is a hazard, so this must be resolved, but it is an unresolved design question rather than a demonstrated impossibility. **Fix:** section drawing of the coupling, per-magnet force spec, and either a mechanical latch or a revised payload rating.

### E-05 â€” AGV envelopes vs zone geometry â€” **High (unresolved)**

The [AGV](https://samsoupsauce.github.io/open-biosphere/hardware/ground-track-agv/) chassis is 350 mm wide Ã— 280 mm tall. The [lower tier](https://samsoupsauce.github.io/open-biosphere/zones/perimeter-lower-tier/) side ribbons are X = [0â€“300 mm] â€” narrower than the AGV specified to run in them â€” and the lowerâ†’mid tier baseline step (0.5 ft â†’ 1.2 ft) is ~215 mm, less than the AGV's 280 mm height. ### E-06 â€” Mantis: clamp-force limits vs support physics â€” **High (unresolved)**

Three unresolved numbers: (a) [RFC-0002](https://samsoupsauce.github.io/open-biosphere/rfcs/rfc-0002-mantis-crawler-kinematics/) and the machine index cap limb clamping at **8.5 N** (a design limit to avoid branch damage, not a measured universal tissue-damage threshold), yet the [mantis stage-0](https://samsoupsauce.github.io/open-biosphere/hardware/mantis-branch-crawler/stage-0/) mechanical clamp specifies "28 N preload" â€” 3.3Ã— that limit. ### E-07 â€” Sprout drum: the specified sheet cannot form the specified drum â€” **High**
