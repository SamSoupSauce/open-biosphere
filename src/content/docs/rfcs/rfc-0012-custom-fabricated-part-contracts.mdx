# RFC-0012: Concrete Custom-Fabricated Part Contracts & Subpage Architecture

* **RFC ID:** `RFC-0012` (Extends and concretizes RFC-0008)
* **Author:** Autonomous Hardware Systems & CAD Engineering Architecture Team
* **Status:** `Implemented / Canonical Standard`
* **Target Classification:** Hardware Architecture / Part Contracts / Documentation Systems
* **Applicable Scope:** All Custom-Fabricated Assemblies, 3D Prints, Machined Adapters & Brackets

---

## 1. Executive Summary & Problem Statement

Prior to RFC-0012, non-standard mechanical interfaces and custom-fabricated assemblies in hardware stage documentation were occasionally deferred to future automated agents (`agy`) or stage authors through vague comments, generic adapter placeholders, or unresolved notes (such as "figure it out at build time" or "custom bracket needed").

In open-source cyber-physical hardware, ambiguous placeholders inflict severe operational liabilities:
* **BOM Rollup Stalls:** Automated procurement tooling and bill-of-materials calculators cannot determine fastener counts, stock material weights, or fabrication costs.
* **Mechanical Planning Desynchronization:** Downstream mechanical subassemblies cannot verify datum alignment, clearance envelopes, or continuous structural load paths without explicit dimensions.
* **Assembly Protocol Breakage:** Human operators and automated assembly rigs cannot proceed past uninstantiated interfaces during zero-power (Stage 0) or manual commissioning.

**RFC-0012** establishes that **every custom physical element required across any Open Biosphere machine must be instantiated immediately as a discrete, named entity conforming to the RFC-0008 schema and hosted at its own dedicated subpage with canonical 3D models and interactive WebGL visualization.**

---

## 2. Core Architectural Directives

### 2.1 Elimination of Ambiguous Placeholders

1. **Zero Unresolved Directives:** Vague comments, inline `TODO` tags, or unresolved directives directed at `agy` or hardware builders are strictly forbidden in machine specifications.
2. **Immediate Instantiation:** If an interface requires custom hardware (such as a tube bracket, weir flume collar, or servo bellcrank), a formal part entity must be declared in the global part registry (`parts.json`) with complete physical metrics, tolerances, material specs, and procurement/fab details.
3. **Food-Safe & Structural Invariants:** Custom parts must strictly adhere to biosphere ecological invariants: zero toxic plastics, food-contact compliance (PETG, PTFE, silicone, 316 stainless steel, 6061 aluminum, borosilicate glass), and continuous corrosion resistance in >85% relative humidity.

### 2.2 Dedicated Subpage Hierarchy Standard

Every custom-fabricated component must reside within its own dedicated directory and route under `parts/`:

```text
src/content/docs/parts/
└── <part-slug>/
    ├── index.mdx              # Technical summary, metadata frontmatter & interactive viewer
    ├── model.gltf              # Canonical glTF 2.0 format geometry asset
    └── assets/                # Supporting engineering figures
        ├── model.gltf               # Canonical glTF 2.0 3D CAD asset
        └── print.stl                # Sliced 3D fabrication geometry
```

* Reused parts across machines or stage levels (e.g., from Stage 0 manual operation through Stage 2 agentic control) link directly to this canonical `<part-slug>` rather than duplicating inline specifications.

### 2.3 Required Subpage Frontmatter Schema

Each `index.mdx` file must declare standardized YAML metadata frontmatter:

```yaml
---
id: "part-custom-<slug>"
title: "<Human-Readable Part Name>"
classification: "custom-fabricated"
fabrication_method: "3d-printed | machined | laser-cut | manual-stock"
material: "aluminum | copper | brass | glass | rubber | petg"
model_file: "./model.gltf"
estimated_cost: 0.00
dimensions:
  x_mm: 0.0
  y_mm: 0.0
  z_mm: 0.0
sidebar:
  badge:
    text: "CUSTOM-FAB"
    variant: "note"
---
```

### 2.4 Required Subpage Sections

1. **Component Overview & Architectural Role:** Narrative explaining the functional duty and why commodity off-the-shelf components cannot satisfy the requirements.
2. **Interactive 3D Inspection:** Embedded `<ModelViewer client:load ... />` component allowing builders to orbit, zoom, toggle wireframe/shaded views, and inspect dimensional bounding boxes directly in the browser.
3. **Technical Summary Contracts:**
   * **Purpose & Functional Role:** Clear engineering rationale.
   * **Mating Interfaces & Tolerances:** Exact mating part IDs, screw thread pitches, and tolerance classes (e.g., ISO 2768-m, H7 reaming).
   * **Fabrication & Post-Processing Instructions:** Slicing settings (wall counts, infill pattern), machining feeds/speeds, tapping protocols, deburring, and surface passivation.
4. **Engineering Blueprints:** Multi-view orthographic dimensional drawings and isometric projections.
5. **Procurement & Sourcing Specification:** Stock material supplier, SKU, direct sourcing link, unit price, MOQ, and lead time.
6. **Reverse Machine Lookup:** Bidirectional traceability table showing all machines and stage levels requiring this component.
